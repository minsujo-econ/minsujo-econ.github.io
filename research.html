Job Market Paper

Large Exporters, Industrial Composition, and Aggregate Exposure to External Shocks

Using monthly Mexican firm-level export data, I show that fluctuations that appear granular at the firm level can have industrial origins. Large exporters experienced larger declines during the COVID-19 trade collapse not because they were more responsive within industries, but because they were disproportionately represented in harder-hit industries. Assigning the top 1% of exporters the industrial composition of the remaining 99% would have eliminated about one-quarter of Mexico’s cumulative export shortfall in 2020Q2. I further show that U.S. demand conditions account for an important part of the cross-industry heterogeneity and that the industrial allocation of large exporters amplified their transmission to aggregate Mexican exports.

The Impact of U.S. Monetary Policy on FDI between Non-U.S. Countries

A structural VAR shows that higher U.S. interest rates reduce Korean FDI in China, providing evidence that U.S. monetary policy affects FDI between non-U.S. countries.
